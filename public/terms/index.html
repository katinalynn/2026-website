Standard Terms of Engagement

Version 1.0 Effective August 1, 2026 inkcapconsulting.com/terms

These terms apply to consulting engagements between Katina L. Rogers, PhD, working through Inkcap Consulting (the Consultant), and the organization retaining her services (the Client). They cover the provisions common to most engagements — payment, intellectual property, confidentiality, and termination — so that individual agreements can stay focused on the work itself.

How these terms are used. A signed proposal, statement of work, contract, or letter of agreement (the Agreement) sets out the scope, schedule, and fee for a particular engagement. These terms are incorporated into that Agreement by reference and supplement it.

Where the Agreement and these terms conflict, the Agreement controls. The version of these terms in effect on the date the Agreement is signed governs that engagement, and later revisions do not apply retroactively.

Working relationship

1.Independent Contractor Status

The Consultant performs services as an independent contractor and not as an employee, partner, or agent of the Client. Nothing in the Agreement or these terms creates an employment relationship.

The Consultant is responsible for her own taxes, insurance, and business expenses, determines the manner and means of performing the work, and may engage subcontractors at her discretion, remaining responsible for their work. Where the Agreement includes budget categories such as fringe or overhead — common in grant-funded work — those categories describe the funder's budget structure and do not constitute employee benefits.

The Consultant may reference her affiliations with other organizations in the course of the work but acts through Inkcap Consulting and not as a representative of any other entity.

Money

2.Fees, Invoicing, and Payment

Fees and the payment schedule are set in the Agreement. Unless the Agreement states otherwise:

  • The Consultant invoices according to the schedule in the Agreement, and invoices are payable within thirty (30) days of receipt.
  • Fees are exclusive of reimbursable expenses, which are addressed in Section 3.
  • Payment is due regardless of whether the Client's own funding, billing, or approval cycles have concluded.

Late payment

Amounts not received within fifteen (15) days of the due date accrue a late fee of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month on the outstanding balance, prorated daily, until paid in full.

If any amount remains outstanding more than sixty (60) days past its due date, the Consultant may suspend work upon written notice until the balance and accrued fees are paid, without prejudice to any other remedy. Any deadline affected by a suspension shifts by the length of the suspension. The Client is responsible for reasonable costs of collection, including attorneys' fees.

Grant-funded engagements

Where the engagement is supported by grant funds, the Client shall notify the Consultant in writing within ten (10) business days of learning that those funds have been reduced, suspended, or withdrawn. The Client remains obligated to pay for all work performed and expenses incurred before the date of that notice. The parties will then confer in good faith about revising scope, schedule, or fee, and any revision will be documented in writing.

3.Expenses and Travel

Travel and other direct costs incurred in performing the work — airfare, lodging, ground transportation, meals, materials — are reimbursed by the Client, either paid in advance or reimbursed within thirty (30) days of an expense request, up to any cap stated in the Agreement.

Expenses above that cap require advance written approval. Expenses are invoiced separately from fees and are not counted against the fee total.

Scope

4.Changes to Scope

The Agreement describes the work to be performed. Anything outside that description is additional work, including:

  • Deliverables, sessions, or meetings beyond the number specified
  • Revision rounds beyond those specified
  • Substantial change in the audience, purpose, or format of a deliverable after work has begun
  • Work required by a material change in the Client's circumstances, personnel, or direction

The Consultant will identify additional work before performing it and provide an estimate of the added fee and any schedule effect. Additional work proceeds only once both parties have agreed in writing, which may include email. Absent a separately negotiated fee, additional work is billed at the Consultant's then-current daily rate.

Small accommodations are part of working well together, and not every adjustment triggers this section. It exists so that larger changes get named early rather than absorbed quietly, and so both parties have a clear way to raise them.

5.Cancellation and Rescheduling

Cancellation of an engagement

If the Client cancels an engagement after the Agreement is signed, the following applies in addition to payment for all work performed and expenses incurred through the cancellation date:

Notice given before the scheduled startCancellation fee
More than 30 days25% of the total fee
30 days or fewer, or after work has begun50% of the total fee

Dated commitments

For engagements built around a specific date — a workshop, talk, facilitation, or convening — the Consultant reserves that date and declines other work for it. If the Client cancels or reschedules a dated commitment:

Notice given before the dateFee due
More than 30 daysNo fee; the session is rescheduled at no charge
15 to 30 days50% of the session fee
Fewer than 15 days100% of the session fee

Non-refundable travel already booked for a cancelled or rescheduled date is reimbursed in full regardless of notice.

Circumstances beyond either party's control

Neither party is liable for delay or cancellation caused by events outside its reasonable control, including illness, bereavement, severe weather, transit failure, or public emergency. In those circumstances the parties will make good-faith efforts to reschedule, and cancellation fees are waived. This provision is available to both parties.

Ownership

6.Intellectual Property

What the Consultant brings

Consultant Materials means everything the Consultant brings to or develops independently of the engagement: methodologies, frameworks, analytical approaches, research instruments, facilitation designs, templates, tools, checklists, rubrics, training materials, and general knowledge, skill, and experience — together with any improvement to them made during the engagement.

Consultant Materials remain the Consultant's property. Nothing in the Agreement transfers them, and nothing restricts the Consultant from using them in other engagements.

What the Client receives

Deliverables means the reports, analyses, written materials, presentations, and other work product the Agreement identifies as deliverables.

Upon receipt of full payment, the Consultant assigns to the Client all right, title, and interest in the Deliverables, excluding any Consultant Materials contained within them. For Consultant Materials embedded in a Deliverable, the Consultant grants the Client a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use, reproduce, adapt, and distribute them as part of that Deliverable in support of the Client's mission.

Before full payment, the Client holds a limited license to use drafts for internal review only. Deliverables may not be published, distributed externally, or put into operational use until payment is complete.

What the Consultant retains

  • Ownership of all Consultant Materials, and the right to use and license them freely
  • The right to describe the engagement in professional portfolios, proposals, biographies, and marketing at a general level — the Client's name, the nature of the work, and its outcomes, excluding confidential information
  • The right to draw on anonymized, aggregated insights from the work in scholarship, teaching, speaking, and writing, in a form that does not identify the Client, its personnel, or any participant without written permission

Third-party and participant materials

Where a Deliverable incorporates material belonging to a third party or contributed by research participants, ownership of that material is unaffected, and it is used according to the terms under which it was provided. Neither party may claim sole ownership of, or sell exclusive rights to, material contributed by participants.

Collaborative and research partnerships. Some engagements — co-equal research-practice partnerships, jointly authored scholarship, co-designed public work — call for shared rather than transferred ownership. Where that is the intent, the Agreement sets out the ownership arrangement directly, and it supersedes this section.

7.Attribution and Publication

Where a Deliverable is published or distributed publicly, the Consultant is attributed by name unless the Agreement provides otherwise.

Where the engagement contemplates jointly authored scholarly or public writing, author order and credit are agreed in writing among the authors in advance of submission or publication.

Information

8.Confidentiality

Each party may receive information the other treats as confidential, including strategic plans, financial data, personnel matters, and unpublished research. Each party will use such information only to perform the Agreement, protect it with at least reasonable care, and not disclose it to others except to those who need it for the engagement and are bound by comparable obligations.

These obligations do not apply to information that is or becomes public without breach, was already known to the receiving party, is independently developed without reference to the disclosed information, or is lawfully received from a third party. Disclosure required by law or court order is permitted, with prompt notice to the other party where the law allows.

Confidentiality obligations continue for three (3) years after the engagement ends. Obligations regarding personal information and research participant data continue indefinitely.

9.Research Involving Human Participants

This section applies where the engagement involves interviews, focus groups, surveys, or other research with human participants.

  • The Client identifies the entity holding Institutional Review Board approval and provides the Consultant with the approved protocol and any subsequent amendments. The Consultant conducts the work in accordance with that protocol.
  • Both parties maintain the confidentiality of participant-identifying information and do not disclose it outside the research team except as the protocol permits or the law requires.
  • Research data is maintained in a shared repository to which the Consultant has access throughout the engagement.
  • Where the engagement contemplates scholarly publication, the Consultant retains access to de-identified data for no fewer than five (5) years after the engagement ends, for the purpose of completing, revising, and responding to peer review. Retention and destruction of identifiable data follow the approved protocol and any applicable funder requirements.

Risk

10.Warranties and Limitation of Liability

What the Consultant warrants

The Consultant warrants that the work will be performed in a professional and workmanlike manner, consistent with the standards of her profession, and that the Deliverables are her original work except for material identified as belonging to a third party. Except as stated here, the work and Deliverables are provided without other warranties, whether express or implied, including implied warranties of merchantability or fitness for a particular purpose.

Decisions remain the Client's

The Consultant's work informs the Client's decisions but does not make them. The Client is responsible for its own decisions, actions, and outcomes, including how it interprets, adopts, or implements any recommendation. The Consultant does not provide legal, financial, medical, or tax advice, and nothing in a Deliverable should be relied upon as such.

Limitation of liability

Neither party is liable to the other for indirect, incidental, special, consequential, or punitive damages, or for lost profits, lost revenue, lost data, or business interruption, whether or not the possibility of such damages was known.

Each party's total aggregate liability arising out of or relating to an Agreement is limited to the total fees paid or payable by the Client under that Agreement.

What the limits do not cover

The exclusions and cap above do not apply to:

  • The Client's obligation to pay fees, expenses, late fees, and cancellation fees
  • Breach of the confidentiality obligations in Section 8
  • Infringement or misappropriation of the other party's intellectual property
  • Gross negligence or willful misconduct
  • Any liability that cannot be limited or excluded under applicable law

These provisions apply equally to both parties and survive the end of the engagement.

Ending the engagement

11.Termination

Either party may terminate an engagement on thirty (30) days' written notice. Upon termination, the Client pays the Consultant for all work performed and expenses incurred through the effective date, on a pro-rata basis, within thirty (30) days of that date. Cancellation fees under Section 5 apply where the Client terminates.

Either party may terminate on fifteen (15) days' written notice for material breach if the breach remains uncured at the end of that period. Non-payment is a material breach.

Sections 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 12 survive termination.

12.Governing Law and General Provisions

These terms and any Agreement incorporating them are governed by the laws of the State of New York. If any provision is found invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions are unaffected.

An Agreement may be amended only in writing, agreed by both parties. Neither party may assign an Agreement without the other's written consent, except to a successor to substantially all of its business.

Notices under these terms are effective when sent by email to the addresses in the Agreement, with confirmation of receipt.

The Agreement, together with these terms and any exhibits, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties on its subject matter and supersedes prior discussions and proposals.
