I am thrilled to announce that for the next eighteen months, I'll be joining the fantastic crew at the [Scholarly Communication Institute](http://bit.ly/GINwnQ)! I'm honored to join [Bethany Nowviskie](http://nowviskie.org/) and her team on the [current phase of SCI's work](http://uvasci.org/current-work/): namely, assessing and rethinking methodological training in the humanities; helping to work on the framework of the stellar [Praxis Program](http://praxis.scholarslab.org/) at the [Scholars' Lab](http://www.scholarslab.org/); and contributing to the continued development of new-model scholarly publications. (For a fuller description, including more detail on the organizations we'll be working with, see [this Scholars' Lab post](http://bit.ly/GWhzYm).)

This new step marks an exciting transition for me. Over the past year, I've worked closely with [Josh Greenberg](http://www.sloan.org/bio/item/44) to develop the Sloan Foundation's budding [Digital Information Technology program](http://www.sloan.org/program/28). In doing so, I've gotten to meet extraordinary people working on innovative projects related changes in scholarly communication in the digital age. In my new position with SCI, I'll be focusing on a number of the same questions, but from a perspective grounded in the humanities. I'm also looking forward to working more deeply on [\#alt-ac](http://mediacommons.futureofthebook.org/alt-ac/) issues, which I deeply care about (as these [two](http://blackinkwhitepage.wordpress.com/2012/01/02/on-scapegoats-opportunities-and-mla-occupation/ "On scapegoats, opportunities, and MLA occupation") [posts](http://blackinkwhitepage.wordpress.com/2012/01/12/alt-ac-moving-toward-a-broader-humanities-community/ "#Alt-ac: Moving toward a broader humanities community") reveal).

It will be an intense 18 months that I'm sure will be over too quickly. I can't wait to dive in!
